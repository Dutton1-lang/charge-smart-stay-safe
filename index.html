Protect your home

Lithium batteries are part of everyday life

Lithium-ion batteries power phones, laptops, power banks, tools, vapes, e-bikes and e-scooters. They are generally safe when used correctly, but damaged batteries, incompatible chargers and unsafe charging can cause rapidly developing fires and toxic smoke.

Most incidents can be reduced through safer buying, charging, storage and disposal.

!

Act on warning signs

Stop using a battery if it becomes unusually hot, swollen, damaged, leaks, smells strange, hisses or smokes.

Battery storage

Simple actions prevent fires

Charge smart

✓ Always

  • Use the charger supplied or recommended by the manufacturer.
  • Charge on a hard, flat and stable surface.
  • Allow batteries to cool before charging.
  • Unplug once charging is complete.
  • Keep smoke alarms working near charging areas.

✕ Never

  • Leave batteries charging while asleep or away from home.
  • Charge on beds, sofas or other soft furnishings.
  • Block an escape route with an e-bike or e-scooter.
  • Use a damaged battery or frayed charger.
  • Modify, open or tamper with a battery pack.

⚠ Warning signs

  • Unusual heat.
  • Swelling or change of shape.
  • Leaking fluid.
  • Hissing, popping or cracking noises.
  • Strong smell, smoke or visible damage.

Travel safely

E-bike and e-scooter safety

01

Buy safely

Use reputable retailers. Check that the battery and charger are intended to work together and meet relevant UK safety requirements.

02

Charge safely

Charge while awake and present. Keep the device away from combustible materials and never charge it on an escape route.

03

Avoid unsafe conversions

Use a competent professional for conversion kits. Do not mix batteries, motors and chargers that are not designed to work together.

04

Register and check recalls

Register products with the manufacturer where possible and act immediately on any safety alert or product recall.

♻

Safe disposal

Never put lithium batteries in household bins

Batteries can be crushed or damaged during waste collection and processing, leading to fires in bin lorries and recycling facilities.

  • Use an approved battery recycling or electrical take-back point.
  • Follow local authority guidance for damaged or swollen batteries.
  • Keep damaged batteries away from heat and combustible materials while seeking advice.

Emergency advice

If a battery catches fire

  1. 1Get everyone out.
  2. 2Close doors behind you if safe.
  3. 3Call 999.
  4. 4Do not attempt to tackle the fire.
  5. 5Stay out and warn others.

Frequently asked questions

Battery safety questions

Can I charge my e-bike overnight?

No. Charge while you are awake and present, and disconnect the charger when charging is complete.

Can I use a different charger?

Use the charger supplied with the product or one specifically recommended by the manufacturer. An incompatible charger can overload the battery.

What should I do with a swollen battery?

Stop using and charging it immediately. Keep it away from heat and combustible materials and seek advice from the manufacturer, retailer or your local authority.

Can lithium batteries go in household recycling?

No. Take them to an approved battery or electrical recycling point.

What should I do if the battery starts smoking?

Leave immediately, close doors if safe, call 999 and do not attempt to tackle the fire.
