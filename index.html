Protect your home from
lithium battery fires

Lithium-ion batteries are used every day in phones, laptops, e-bikes, e-scooters, power tools and many other rechargeable devices.

They are safe when used correctly, but if damaged, incorrectly charged or poor quality they can fail without warning.

A lithium battery fire develops rapidly, burns at extremely high temperatures and produces toxic smoke.

Most fires involving lithium batteries occur while the battery is charging.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

Can I charge my e-bike overnight?

No. We strongly advise against charging e-bikes or e-scooters overnight while you are asleep, as you will not be alerted immediately if a thermal failure occurs.

Is it safe to use third party chargers?

Always use the manufacturer-approved charger. Third-party chargers may not have the correct voltage or safety cut-offs designed for your specific battery.

What should I do with a swollen battery?

Stop using and charging it immediately. Place it outdoors away from combustible materials if safe to do so, and contact local recycling or waste services for hazardous disposal advice.

Can lithium batteries explode?

If damaged or overcharged, lithium batteries can enter thermal runaway, rapidly building up pressure and bursting into high-temperature flames.

How do I recycle lithium batteries?

Never throw them in general waste bins. Take them to designated battery recycling banks found at supermarkets or household waste recycling centers.
